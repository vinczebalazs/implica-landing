The best news apps in 2026

There is no single best news app, only the one that fits how you want to follow the news. Here are nine worth knowing, what each does well, what it costs, and the catch.

Updated 22 Oct 2026 By Implica 10 min read

THE SHORT ANSWER

For a free, broad feed, use Google News. For paywalled magazines and newspapers on an iPhone, use Apple News+. To see how different outlets cover the same story, use Ground News or AllSides. For quick AI summaries, use Particle. For news near you, use NewsBreak. And if you want each story explained rather than just reported, try Implica, though we make it, so take that with a pinch of salt.

What counts as a news app here

This guide covers apps that bring together news from many outlets. It leaves out apps from a single publisher (CNN, the BBC, the New York Times), RSS readers, newsletters and social media. Those can all be good ways to follow the news, but they solve a different problem.

The list is not ranked. The apps differ too much for a single order to mean anything: a local-news app and a media-bias app aren't competing for the same reader. Each one is labelled with what it's good for instead. Prices are US prices as listed in October 2026. They change, and they vary by country, so check the App Store or Google Play before you subscribe.

Google News

GOOD FOR A free, broad view of the day

Google News is the default for a reason: it's free, it covers almost every outlet, and it works everywhere. The For you tab builds a feed from the topics, places and publications you follow and from your reading history. Headlines shows the day's biggest stories for everyone.

Its most useful feature is Full Coverage. Tap it on any story and you get the same event as reported by many outlets, with related videos, analysis and a timeline. Unlike the For you tab, Full Coverage isn't personalised, so everyone sees the same set of sources.

Google News is a directory of links, though. Tapping a story takes you to the publisher's site, with its paywall, pop-ups and ads, and the app adds little explanation of its own.

PROS

  • Free, with a huge range of sources
  • Full Coverage shows one story from many outlets
  • Good local and topic following

CONS

  • Personalisation is tied to your Google account and activity
  • Sends you out to publisher sites and their paywalls
  • Endless feed; easy to keep scrolling

Price: Free.

Apple News

GOOD FOR Paywalled magazines and newspapers

Apple News comes pre-installed on iPhones, iPads and Macs. The free version is a clean, well-edited headline feed. The reason to use it is Apple News+, which bundles hundreds of magazines and several major newspapers into one subscription, along with audio versions of stories and daily puzzles.

If you'd otherwise pay for two or three of the publications it includes, News+ pays for itself. If you mostly read free news, the free tier is enough. Apple News+ is also part of the Apple One Premier bundle.

PROS

  • One subscription for many paywalled titles
  • Clean design, with stories read inside the app
  • Audio stories for commutes

CONS

  • Apple devices only; nothing for Android
  • News+ is only sold in a few countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia)
  • Value depends entirely on which titles you'd read

Price: Free; Apple News+ is $12.99 a month in the US, with a free first month for new subscribers.

Ground News

GOOD FOR Seeing how outlets across the spectrum cover a story

Ground News groups articles about the same event and shows how coverage splits across left, centre and right-leaning outlets. Its Blindspot section surfaces stories covered mostly by one side, which is a quick way to notice what your usual sources skip. Each outlet also carries ratings for bias, factuality and ownership.

The bias labels are judgments drawn from third-party rating services, and they're framed around the US political spectrum. They work best as a prompt to read more widely, not as a verdict. The full comparison tools sit behind the paid plans.

PROS

  • Makes coverage gaps visible at a glance
  • Bias, factuality and ownership info per outlet
  • Works on phone and web

CONS

  • Most useful features are paid
  • Bias ratings are judgments and US-centric
  • Tells you who covered a story, not what it means

Price: Free tier. Paid plans run from Pro (about $1 a month in the app) to Vantage at $99.99 a year on the web.

Flipboard

GOOD FOR A magazine-style feed built around your interests

Flipboard turns news into a magazine you flip through. You pick topics, from world news to photography to cooking, and follow curated "magazines" made by editors and other readers. It now also lets you follow accounts from the fediverse, the network that includes Mastodon and Threads, inside the app.

It's at its best for interests beyond the headlines. As a way to keep up with the day's essential news it's less focused, and the feed is only as good as the time you spend tuning it.

PROS

  • Pleasant, visual reading experience
  • Great for hobbies and niche interests
  • Can follow Mastodon and Threads accounts

CONS

  • Needs setup to be useful
  • Ad-supported
  • Lots of lifestyle content mixed in with news

Price: Free.

SmartNews

GOOD FOR Fast, free headlines plus local tabs

SmartNews organises news into tabs (top stories, US, world, business, entertainment, local and more) that you swipe between. It loads quickly, needs no account, and covers a wide range of outlets. It's especially popular in the US and Japan.

The trade-off is the usual one for free apps: plenty of ads, and most stories open the publisher's page instead of explaining anything in the app.

PROS

  • Free and fast, no sign-up
  • Handy local, weather and traffic tabs
  • Wide source coverage

CONS

  • Ad-heavy
  • So many tabs it can feel cluttered
  • Headlines first, context rarely

Price: Free, ad-supported.

Particle

GOOD FOR AI summaries of stories from many outlets

Particle, built by former Twitter engineers, collects articles about a story from many outlets and writes a short AI summary of them. You can switch how a story is presented, for example a simpler explanation or the opposing sides of an argument. Since early 2026 it also pulls relevant clips from podcasts into stories. It started on iPhone and is now on Android and the web too.

It's quick, and good for skimming many stories. As with any AI summary, the details are worth checking against the original reporting when they matter.

PROS

  • Fast summaries that draw on many sources
  • Different "views" of a story
  • Podcast clips alongside the text

CONS

  • AI summaries can miss nuance or get details wrong
  • Summary-first: less background on why a story matters
  • Some features need Particle+

Price: Free; Particle+ is $2.99 a month or $29.99 a year.

NewsBreak

GOOD FOR Local news

NewsBreak is built around where you live. It pulls in local newspapers, TV stations and community sources, and adds alerts for things like weather and crime nearby. Local coverage is hard to find in one place anywhere else, which is its main appeal.

Source quality varies a lot, from established local papers to small contributors, so it's worth checking who wrote a story before sharing it.

PROS

  • Strongest local coverage of any app here
  • Useful nearby alerts
  • Free

CONS

  • Uneven source quality
  • Lots of ads unless you pay
  • Mostly US-focused

Price: Free; ad-free premium plans start at $4.99.

AllSides

GOOD FOR Left, centre and right side by side

AllSides takes the big stories of the day and shows a left, a centre and a right-leaning headline next to each other, with a short neutral summary. It's also the source of widely used media bias ratings, which it publishes for hundreds of outlets.

It's a quick daily habit for anyone who wants to step outside one viewpoint. Seeing three framings shows you the disagreement, but it doesn't always explain the background you need to judge it.

PROS

  • Free, with no in-app purchases
  • Three perspectives on each big story
  • Transparent bias ratings

CONS

  • Covers fewer stories than the big aggregators
  • Built around US politics
  • Framing, not explanation

Price: Free.

How to choose

Start with the question you want a news app to answer:

  • "What's happening?" Google News or SmartNews. Free, broad, fast.
  • "What do the big papers and magazines say?" Apple News+, if you're on Apple devices.
  • "Am I only hearing one side?" Ground News or AllSides.
  • "Give me the gist, quickly." Particle.
  • "What's happening near me?" NewsBreak.
  • "I want to follow my interests, not just the news." Flipboard.
  • "Why does this matter, and how did we get here?" Implica.

Many people use two: one broad, free app for everything, and one that does a single job well. Most of the paid apps above offer a free tier or trial, so it's easy to try a few for a week and keep the one you actually open.

Questions people ask

What is the best free news app?

Google News is the most complete free option, with a huge range of sources, Full Coverage, and local news. SmartNews, Flipboard and AllSides are also free. Apple News is free on Apple devices, though its best content needs News+.

What is the most unbiased news app?

No app is entirely free of bias, because choosing which stories to show is itself a judgment. Ground News and AllSides are built specifically to show how coverage differs across the political spectrum, which is the most practical way to balance your reading. See also Ground News alternatives.

What's the difference between a news app and a news aggregator?

A news aggregator collects stories from many outlets in one place; every app in this guide is one. Our guide to the five types of news aggregator explains how they differ. A publisher's own app, such as the BBC's or the New York Times', only shows that publisher's journalism.

Are AI news summaries reliable?

They're good for getting the gist quickly, but they can miss nuance or occasionally get details wrong. Use apps that link to the original reporting, and check the source when a detail matters.

Is Apple News+ worth it?

It's worth it if you'd otherwise subscribe to two or more of the magazines or newspapers it includes. If you mostly read free news, the free version of Apple News, or any of the free apps above, will do. We go through it in detail in is Apple News+ worth it?
