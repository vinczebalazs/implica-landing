THE SHORT ANSWER

On Android and want something like Apple News? Try Flipboard for the magazine feel, or Google News for the widest free coverage. Leaving Google News? Use an RSS reader like Feedly or Inoreader for control without an algorithm, Ground News or AllSides for balance, or Particle for AI summaries. Tired of feeds altogether? Implica, which we make, gives you a short, explained set of the day's important stories instead.

Is Apple News available on Android?

No. Apple News and Apple News+ only run on iPhone, iPad and Mac. There's no Android app and no full web version, and that's unlikely to change. So if you've switched from iPhone to Android, or you share a household with both, you'll need something else.

Why people leave Google News

Google News is free and has an enormous range of sources, but the reasons people give for leaving come up again and again:

  • Privacy. Its personalised feed draws on your Google account and activity.
  • It's a list of links. Stories send you to publishers' sites, with their paywalls, pop-ups and ads.
  • It never ends. An endless feed makes it easy to keep scrolling long after you're informed.

Flipboard

GOOD FOR Apple News's magazine feel, on any phone

Flipboard is the closest in spirit to Apple News: a polished, visual, flip-through layout, with topic "magazines" curated by editors and readers. It's on iPhone, Android and the web, and it can also follow Mastodon and Threads accounts. It's ad-supported and needs some setup to get a good feed. Price: free.

Google News

GOOD FOR The widest free coverage on Android

For Apple News users moving to Android, Google News is the obvious default. It's free, pre-installed on many Android phones, and covers almost every outlet. Its Full Coverage view, which shows any story across many outlets, is one of its best features. Price: free.

SmartNews

GOOD FOR Fast headlines, no account needed

SmartNews works on both iPhone and Android and needs no sign-in, which suits people who want neither Apple's nor Google's ecosystem. Its swipeable tabs cover top stories, world, business and local news. It's ad-heavy. Price: free.

Feedly and Inoreader (RSS readers)

GOOD FOR Your chosen sources, with no algorithm

If what bothers you about Google News is the algorithm, an RSS reader removes it. You pick the sites, and the reader shows every new article from them in order. Feedly's free plan follows up to 100 sources, and Inoreader's up to 150. On Apple devices, NetNewsWire is a free, open-source option. Read more in our guide to the five types of news aggregator. Price: free plans; paid tiers from about $6 a month.

Ground News and AllSides

GOOD FOR Seeing coverage from every side

Both group stories and show how outlets on the left, centre and right cover them. AllSides is free. Ground News has a free tier and paid plans for its full features. See our guide to Ground News alternatives for more options of this kind. Price: free tiers.

Particle

GOOD FOR AI summaries from many outlets

Particle condenses many articles about a story into one AI summary, so you read the gist in the app instead of clicking out to publishers' sites. It's on iPhone, Android and the web. Check the original reporting when details matter. Price: free; Particle+ $2.99 a month.

Implica (ours, so we're biased)

GOOD FOR The important stories, explained, on iPhone and Android

We make Implica, so take this with a pinch of salt. Instead of a feed of links, it gives you a short, curated set of the day's most important stories, and each one is explained in the app: what happened, why it matters and who it affects, the background, and what to watch next. Tap any term you don't know for a quick explanation, or ask a question about the story. It works the same on iPhone and Android, needs no account, and lists the sources each story draws on. It uses AI to summarise and explain, so check important details against those sources. It covers world, tech and science news, plus a local edition for Hungary in Hungarian and English, with more local editions coming. Price: free for 14 days, then a subscription.

Questions people ask

What is the Android equivalent of Apple News?

Google News is the default equivalent and is free on Android. If you liked Apple News's magazine-style design more than its coverage, Flipboard is closer in feel.

Can I read Apple News on a Windows PC or the web?

Not in full. Apple News has no full web reader or Windows app; it's built for Apple devices.

What's the most private alternative to Google News?

An RSS reader like NetNewsWire has no account, no tracking and no algorithm. Among news apps, ones that work without signing in, like SmartNews or Implica, at least don't tie your reading to a personal account.
