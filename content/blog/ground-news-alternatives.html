What Ground News does, and why people look elsewhere

Ground News groups articles about the same event and shows how coverage splits across outlets rated left, centre and right. It flags "Blindspot" stories covered mostly by one side, and shows bias, factuality and ownership information for each outlet.

People look for alternatives for a few common reasons:

  • Price. The most useful features are paid, and the top plan, Vantage, is $99.99 a year on the web.
  • The bias labels. They're judgments drawn from third-party rating services and built around US politics, which fits international news less well.
  • It shows coverage, not meaning. You see who covered a story, but you still click out to publishers' sites, with their paywalls, to understand it.

AllSides

GOOD FOR Left, centre and right side by side, free

AllSides is the most direct alternative. For each big story it shows a left, a centre and a right-leaning headline next to each other, with a short neutral summary. Its media bias ratings for hundreds of outlets are widely used. Ground News itself draws on them. It covers fewer stories than Ground News and is just as US-focused, but it costs nothing.

Price: Free, with no in-app purchases.

Google News (Full Coverage)

GOOD FOR Many outlets on one story, free

Open any story in Google News and tap Full Coverage. You'll see the event as reported by many different outlets, plus analysis, opinion pieces and a timeline. It doesn't label outlets by political lean, but the range of sources is far wider than any bias tool, and the Full Coverage view isn't personalised, so everyone sees the same sources.

Price: Free.

Particle

GOOD FOR AI summaries with an opposing-sides view

Particle reads many articles about a story and writes an AI summary. It lets you switch views, including one that lays out the opposing sides of an issue. It's quick, but as with any AI summary, check the original reporting when details matter.

Price: Free; Particle+ is $2.99 a month or $29.99 a year.

The Flip Side

GOOD FOR Left and right opinion on one issue a day

The Flip Side is a free daily email newsletter founded in 2017. Each edition picks one issue and quotes editorials and commentary from both left-leaning and right-leaning outlets. It's a good fit if you want the arguments rather than the headlines, in a few minutes a day.

Price: Free, with optional paid support.

Tangle

GOOD FOR Arguments from all sides, with a clear take

Tangle is a politics newsletter by journalist Isaac Saul. Each edition summarises what the left and the right are saying about one story, then gives Saul's own view, clearly labelled. Weekday editions are free, and paid subscribers get extra editions.

Price: Free weekday editions; paid membership for extras.

Ad Fontes Media

GOOD FOR Checking an outlet's bias and reliability

Ad Fontes Media publishes the Media Bias Chart, which plots news outlets on two axes: political lean and reliability. It's a reference rather than a news feed: useful when you come across an unfamiliar source and want a quick read on it.

Price: Free to browse.

Questions people ask

Is there a free version of Ground News?

Yes. Ground News has a free tier, but its most useful features, such as the full bias breakdowns and Blindspot, sit behind paid plans. AllSides and Google News Full Coverage are fully free alternatives.

Is Ground News biased?

Ground News doesn't write news. It labels outlets using ratings from third-party services. Those ratings are judgments, and they're framed around US politics, so it's best to treat them as a guide rather than a verdict.

What is the most unbiased news source?

No source is entirely free of bias, because choosing which stories to cover is itself a judgment. Wire services such as the Associated Press and Reuters are widely regarded as among the most straightforward, and reading across outlets with tools like AllSides is the most reliable way to balance your news.
